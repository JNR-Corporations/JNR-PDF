Understanding PDF security

What AES-256 does

Your PDF is encrypted with AES-256 by a WebAssembly-based qpdf toolkit. A correct opening password is needed to view its contents in compatible readers.

What permissions really do

Print, edit, and copy are standard PDF permission flags that compatible readers respect. They cannot guarantee that a recipient who can open the document will never extract, screenshot, or otherwise capture content.

Why there are two passwords

The opening password grants normal reading access subject to permissions. The distinct owner/admin key lets you administer the protected PDF. An automatically generated owner key is used unless you enter your own.

Local-only processing

Files and passwords do not go to a JNR server. On first use, browser downloads the qpdf WebAssembly module and toolkit from a CDN. Use an up-to-date browser and a trusted device. The page has no analytics or tracking scripts.

Compatibility and preservation

Existing encrypted PDFs require their valid current password. Some PDF applications cannot read AES-256 documents. Encryption rewrites the PDF structure and may invalidate pre-existing digital signatures. Optional flattening or attachment removal changes the document intentionally.

Protect your keys

Neither password is recoverable through this page. Save a copy in your trusted password manager or another secure place. Never share the owner key unless you intend to grant administrator access.
