Know what you are agreeing to.

These documents are written for the JNR PDFs / JNR PDF Studio website and its related PDF utilities. Some tools may be hosted on different routes or providers, so the tool page and its actual processing behaviour matter as much as this central page.

Important file-handling reminder

Do not assume every tool processes files only on your device. Processing may be browser-based, server-based or provided by an external service, depending on the specific tool. Before uploading a sensitive document, check that tool’s notice. If the page does not explain where processing happens, avoid using it for confidential or high-risk files.

Privacy Policy

Core policyApplies to website use

Explains what information may be handled, why it may be used, who may process it, and how to raise a privacy request.

Read full privacy policy

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the JNR PDFs / JNR PDF Studio public website, its document-tool pages maintained by JNR Corporations, and related support communications that link to this policy. A third-party website or separately operated tool may have its own controller/operator and policy; that separate policy applies to its handling of information.

Service operator shown on this site: JNR Corporations. The operator should confirm its full legal name, business address (if required), and privacy-contact details before publishing this page as a final legal notice.

2. Information that may be handled

  • Information you submit: an email address or message you send for help, feedback, complaints or rights requests; files and settings you intentionally provide to a tool; and information you include in a form.
  • Basic technical information: request timestamps, browser/device information, approximate network information, error reports and security logs that may be generated by the hosting or delivery infrastructure.
  • On-device preferences: the website may use browser storage to remember a preference or whether an installation prompt has been shown or dismissed.
  • Usage/diagnostic data: information from analytics, monitoring or error tools only where those tools are actually enabled in the deployed version. Do not treat this policy as confirmation that an unlisted analytics product is active.

3. Why information may be used

Information may be used to deliver a requested PDF operation, respond to support messages, maintain site functionality, troubleshoot errors, prevent abuse, protect the service, document a privacy request, meet legal requirements and improve reliability. The purpose should be limited to what is relevant to the feature or communication involved.

4. Documents and personal data inside files

PDFs can contain personal or sensitive details. Handling depends on the particular tool’s architecture: a browser-only operation may process a file on your device, while another operation may send content to a server, hosted library, conversion provider or another website. A tool-specific notice should tell you which applies. JNR PDFs does not promise that all documents remain on-device unless the specific tool has been verified to do so.

Only upload documents you are authorised to use. Remove unnecessary sensitive information before processing and retain your own safe backup of important files.

5. Legal grounds and choices

Where required by applicable law, processing should be based on a valid legal ground, including consent when required, performance of a requested service, compliance with legal obligations or another ground permitted by law. You may be able to withdraw consent, object, request access or correction, or ask for deletion, subject to applicable law and any lawful retention requirement.

6. Sharing and service providers

Information may be available to infrastructure providers, hosting/CDN providers, security or diagnostics providers, and third-party conversion services only to the extent they are actually used and needed to operate a relevant feature. It may also be disclosed when required by law, to address abuse or security incidents, or to protect rights and safety. The operator should maintain an accurate, current list of providers and their roles before production publication.

Personal data is not intended to be sold as a standalone product. This does not mean that service providers never process technical data to deliver hosting, security or another requested feature.

7. International handling

A hosting or software provider may process technical information in a country other than your own. Actual locations depend on the provider configuration. Where applicable, transfers should follow relevant legal requirements and suitable safeguards.

8. Security

Reasonable technical and organisational safeguards should be used in proportion to the type of information handled. No website, network or file-transfer method can be promised perfectly secure. Do not submit high-risk information unless the relevant tool provides sufficient, verified handling details for your needs.

9. Changes to this policy

The current version and update date appear in the Version History section. Material changes should be described clearly. Continued use after an update may be subject to the revised policy where permitted by law; consent will be requested again when legally required.

PDF File Processing & Upload Notice

Before upload

The practical guide to checking where files go, what output to verify, and how to handle sensitive documents.

Read file-handling notice

Before choosing a tool

  1. Read the processing note shown on that tool’s page.
  2. Check whether the tool says “processed in your browser,” “uploaded to our server,” or “handled by an external service.” Do not infer one from the design alone.
  3. Review any file-size, file-type and output-quality limitations before uploading.
  4. Keep an original copy of your file until you have checked the exported output.

After processing

Use the tool’s explicit remove/clear control if provided. Also review your downloads folder and browser downloads history on shared devices. Clearing a webpage does not necessarily remove a file already downloaded or any server-side copy that a tool may have created.

High-risk files: Avoid uploading government IDs, medical records, financial statements, legal evidence, employer-confidential files or documents belonging to another person unless you are authorised and understand the complete processing path.

Output checking

Conversion may affect layout, fonts, links, forms, signatures, OCR text or accessibility. Check the exported file before filing, publishing, signing or relying on it for a legal or business decision.

Cookies, Local Storage & Cache Notice

Browser controls

Explains browser memory used for preferences and technical operation, including the PWA installation prompt and service-worker assets.

Read storage notice

The website’s current page code includes browser local storage for remembering installation-prompt state. The site also registers a service worker, which can cache website assets depending on the worker’s implementation. Browsers, hosting providers and security infrastructure may use other technical storage that depends on deployment.

These items are not necessarily the same as advertising cookies. This page does not claim that third-party analytics or advertising cookies are active. The live deployment should be inspected periodically and this section updated whenever new analytics, marketing, embedded media or consent tools are added.

Managing storage

  • Use your browser’s site settings to inspect or remove cookies and site data.
  • Clearing site data may reset saved preferences, PWA installation state and cached assets.
  • Blocking required storage or service-worker functions may affect installation, updates or some website features.

Where applicable law requires consent for non-essential storage, the operator should implement a compliant choice before activating that storage.

Retention & Deletion

Data lifecycle

Explains what can be deleted by you, what may remain in browser storage, and why no universal server-retention period is promised here.

Read retention and deletion rules

Retention depends on the tool and the systems involved. Browser-only tools may leave downloaded outputs on your device or temporary data in browser memory. A server-based tool may create temporary or persistent copies; its page should state the relevant retention window. Hosting providers may retain security logs for limited periods to operate and protect the service.

This central page does not specify a single number of hours or days for all files because the current deployment and every tool’s storage configuration must be verified before such a promise can be made.

How to request deletion

  1. Send a message to the privacy contact with the relevant page/tool, approximate date and the information you want removed.
  2. Do not attach the sensitive document unless it is necessary to identify the record and you understand the risk.
  3. The operator should verify the request proportionately, identify any system it controls, and respond within the period required by applicable law.

Deletion may be limited where information is held by a third party, already downloaded by you, retained in protected backups for a limited period, or required to be preserved by law. Any limitation should be explained where possible.

Security & Responsible Disclosure

Report a concern

How to report a suspected security issue or privacy incident without accessing or exposing other users’ data.

Read security reporting policy

How to report

Contact the listed privacy/support email with the subject “Security Report”. Include the affected page or route, a plain-language summary, the potential impact and safe reproduction information. Avoid sending real user documents, credentials, personal data or screenshots that expose third parties.

Responsible research boundaries

  • Only test systems you own or have explicit permission to assess.
  • Do not access, alter, download, retain or disclose other people’s data.
  • Do not interrupt service, run destructive tests or publicly disclose an unpatched issue before coordinating with the operator.
  • Stop testing once you have enough information to demonstrate the issue safely.

This page does not create a bug-bounty programme, promise payment, authorise testing, or guarantee a specific response time. The operator should triage reports, limit access to incident data, remediate verified issues and make notifications required by applicable law.
